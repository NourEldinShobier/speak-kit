The quiet return of local-first software

For a decade, the default answer to every software question was the cloud. Notes, documents and even voices lived on someone else's server, rented back to us by the month. Lately that answer has started to wobble.

The appeal is simple. When the work happens on your own machine, it keeps working on a plane, in a tunnel, and long after a startup has changed its pricing. Nothing leaves the device, so there is nothing to leak.

Why now

Small models now run on an ordinary laptop, and they sound good enough that nobody asks where the audio came from. That changes what software can promise its users.
